Data, definitions & limitations

Source: Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED), U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics CPI series, downloaded and aggregated to annual averages. The underlying data file contains 260 rows: one CPI category in one calendar year, 2000–2025. Each category's index is rebased to 100 in 2000 using 100 × (annual CPI / category's 2000 annual CPI). No category-year observations are excluded from the prepared file.

The four summary numbers are: (1) the unweighted arithmetic mean of ten category price changes from 2000 to 2025; (2) the largest category price change; (3) the smallest category price change; and (4) the number of distinct categories. Each category's change is 100 × (index at end / index at start − 1). The same formulas are used in the dashboard, with user-selected dates and categories.

Interpretation: These are changes in prices, not changes in affordability adjusted for income. The categories are not mutually exclusive (e.g. childcare and tuition may be components of broader price baskets), and their simple average is not an official aggregate CPI. Series may use different seasonal-adjustment conventions in the source export; annual averaging reduces, but does not eliminate, comparability concerns. Childcare's unusually small change deserves verification against the exact FRED series definition before drawing substantive conclusions.
